What we heard

142 comments arrived between June 12 and August 7 — by email, at two committee meetings, and on paper forms left at the Sherwood Recreation Center. Staff grouped them into four themes. Names are withheld; the full unedited record is available on request.

Traffic safety

Raised in 88 comments

The most common theme by a wide margin. Residents described near-misses at the 15th and A Street NE intersection and cited the two crashes involving cyclists in the past three years. Parents of school-age children wrote most often.

Parking loss

Raised in 61 comments

The strongest objection. Households without off-street parking, and residents who described a disability or care-giving arrangement, said the 34 removed spaces would fall hardest on them. Several asked for a resident-only block instead.

Cost and timing

Raised in 29 comments

Residents asked what the project costs, whether it competes with the long-delayed sidewalk repairs on 12th Street, and how long 15th Street would be torn up. A number of comments were not objections so much as requests for a schedule.

Official response

Recommended by the Transportation & Public Space Committee on August 28, 2026. It becomes the Commission’s official response when the full commission votes on October 8.

Proceed
Support the project, with two conditions
Committee vote: 4 in favor, 1 opposed, 0 abstaining

The safety record on this corridor is the deciding fact. Two crashes involving cyclists in three years, on a street that two schools sit beside, is a pattern the Commission cannot answer with paint. The committee finds that a curb-protected lane addresses the harm residents described most often and most urgently.

The parking objection is real and is not dismissed. The committee therefore conditions its support on two things: that DDOT designate at least three accessible parking spaces per block between Independence and H Street, and that one marked loading zone be added on each commercial block. Without both conditions the committee’s recommendation reverts to Not yet.

Next step
The full commission votes on this recommendation at the October 8 public meeting.

Public comment is taken at the top of the agenda, two minutes per speaker, no sign-up required. Written comment is accepted until 5:00 pm the day before. Commissioner Whitfield will read the two conditions aloud before the vote so they are recorded verbatim.
